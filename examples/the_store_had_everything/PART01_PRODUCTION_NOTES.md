## Why the opening should be tested first

The first paragraph contains the title promise, the resource-rich location, the ironic public-address threat, the two-character objective, and a deadline. It therefore tests the actual launch hook rather than an unrelated style sample.

The pilot should include the storefront, Nolan and Lena behind checkout three, and the distant security office. These must be locked as one continuous physical setup. No arbitrary change in their positions, the infected crowd, or the front-store landmarks is permitted between pilot frames.

## Continuity-sensitive moments in this draft

1. **Checkout three to camping:** Nolan leads; Lena follows with van keys. The radio is acquired at the camping display and must remain with her afterward.
2. **Blue-cooler hiding setup:** infected man approaches toward the front; Nolan and Lena move off the direct aisle behind the coolers. Orange tent poles and the green kayak display remain fixed landmarks.
3. **Dropped keys:** the keys fall, the infected turns, the next announcement redirects him, and Lena retrieves the keys before leaving. No later frame may omit either the keys or radio.
4. **Rear stairs and office entry:** both characters move from the camping route to the elevated office. The radio crank is held still during the quiet keyhole moment.
5. **Control panel:** the building/control systems are not all switched off. Only the public-address amplifier is disabled; cameras and shutter controls remain available.
6. **Shutter sequence:** the shutter descends once and locks. Later front-camera frames must show the shutter, not return to uncovered glass.
7. **Office ending:** the radio is on the desk, Lena has the keys, the van is nose-out in the loading bay, and the front shutter has a persistent inward deformation after the bang.

These become scene-state records and establishing-master requirements during visual planning. See `CONTINUITY_PROTOCOL.md`.
