# The Store Had Everything — Rough Series Arc

Status: draft for user review, 2026-10-23. Part 1 is fully drafted in `PART01_SCRIPT.md`; later parts remain flexible until the opening pilot and Part 1 are approved.

## Story promise

Two ordinary people are locked inside an outdoor-supply superstore during an infected outbreak. The building contains almost everything they could need, but its cheerful automated announcements keep drawing infected toward the glass.

This is not a story about an expert teaching a beginner. It is about two people with incomplete knowledge, different instincts, and one building that makes staying and leaving equally tempting.

## Protagonists

### Nolan Brooks — night stock clerk

- Twenty-six, observant, tired, and prone to overthinking when a decision becomes irreversible.
- Knows the aisles, staff corridors, security-office key, shutter controls, and where stock is kept. That makes him useful, not omniscient.
- His first instinct is to trust the building because it is familiar and full of supplies.
- His arc is toward making decisions without waiting for complete certainty.

### Lena Ortiz — delivery driver

- Thirty-four, direct, physically capable from ordinary work, and frightened without pretending otherwise.
- Has the keys to her delivery van in the rear loading bay and is used to route changes, closed roads, and incomplete delivery information.
- Her first instinct is to preserve a way to move rather than become dependent on one building.
- Her arc is toward understanding that movement is useful only when it has a viable destination.

Neither character gives survival lessons. Each notices things the other misses, makes mistakes, and has to revise a choice.

## Fictional outbreak rules

These are story rules, not real disease guidance:

- Infected orient strongly toward recurring or sudden human-made sound.
- A sound can attract attention; it does not give infected supernatural knowledge of exactly where a hidden person is.
- Silence can reduce new attention, but it does not make an existing crowd disappear.
- Infected are dangerous through persistence, numbers, and pressure against barriers. They are not superhumanly strong.
- The origin of the outbreak is not explained through invented science in Part 1.
- Violence remains non-graphic; suspense comes from timing, space, choices, and consequences.

## Fixed store geography for continuity

These locations must keep the same relationships in every script, reference, and frame unless a later approved story event changes them:

1. **Front storefront and checkouts:** glass entrance along the front wall; checkout lanes inside; blue night beyond the glass. The powered metal shutter descends outside the interior checkout area.
2. **Central camping department:** fixed orange tent display, blue cooler stack, and green kayak wall form recognizable landmarks. The characters use this route after the direct aisle becomes unsafe.
3. **Rear security office:** elevated one short flight above the rear sales floor, with an interior observation window, monitors, public-address controls, and shutter controls. It is not a basement or a front-office room.
4. **Rear stockroom and loading bay:** beyond the sales floor and separate from the security-office entrance. Lena's van is parked in the bay with its nose toward the exterior loading shutter.
5. **Exits:** front entrance, rear loading shutter, and one alarmed side fire exit. A frame must not invent a convenient extra exit.

A measured floor plan, camera map, and location masters will be created during reference production. See `CONTINUITY_PROTOCOL.md`.

## Four-part arc

### Part 1 — The Last Announcement

**Immediate goal:** cross the sales floor and stop the public-address announcements.

Nolan and Lena discover that the thirty-second announcement cycle both endangers them and briefly gives them cover to move. An infected already inside the store forces them to test that rhythm at close range. They reach the security office, stop only the announcement system while preserving cameras and exit lighting, and lower the front shutter.

**Payoff:** the cheerful voice stops and the front glass is protected.

**New problem:** an emergency radio gives them official information but no risk-free answer. Lena's van is visible in the loading bay while the front shutter begins to deform under the crowd. They must choose between reinforcing a supply-rich building and preparing to leave.

### Part 2 — The Van in the Bay

**Immediate goal:** prepare for either choice, then reach the van if staying becomes untenable.

The characters assemble small mobile kits rather than attempting to carry the store with them: water, compact nonperishable food, first aid, light, batteries, the radio, warm layers, and essential personal items. These choices occur while a specific deadline closes, not as a shopping montage.

A failing front barrier changes the decision for them. Reaching the loading bay requires crossing the stockroom without using the same announcement rhythm they disabled in Part 1. The van offers mobility, but Lena checks its actual condition and the available route instead of treating it as a magical rescue.

**Planned payoff:** a difficult departure or a deliberate last-second decision not to depart, determined by the danger and verified route information.

### Part 3 — The Closed Road

**Immediate goal:** turn movement into a viable destination.

Road closures, conflicting rumours, and an official route update prevent a simple drive to safety. Lena's route knowledge matters, but she cannot predict the outbreak. Nolan's store knowledge is largely gone, forcing both characters to adapt without either becoming an instant expert.

The mobile-kit choices from Part 2 pay off. Anything they failed to bring must remain absent; supplies cannot appear when convenient.

**Planned payoff:** they reach a plausible shelter or evacuation point, but only after abandoning a plan that no longer fits the evidence.

### Part 4 — The Last Delivery

**Immediate goal:** survive the consequences of the route decision and reach other people without repeating the store's attention trap.

The final part resolves the central tension between shelter and movement. The remaining supplies have specific uses and limits. Lena completes a very different kind of delivery, while Nolan uses what he has learned about systems, entrances, and timing without becoming a commander.

**Intended ending:** costly but hopeful, with the immediate ordeal resolved and the larger outbreak not falsely declared over.

## Practical-information spine

Real emergency information will remain attached to decisions:

- Part 1: use a battery/hand-crank radio for information when normal communications fail; preserve necessary building systems rather than indiscriminately cutting power; make shelter/evacuation decisions from actual conditions and official information.
- Part 2: a small, portable emergency kit is more useful during movement than an unmanageable pile of supplies.
- Part 3: routes and instructions can change; verify information and abandon a route that has become more dangerous.
- Part 4: supplies, communication, and a clear next action matter more than a dramatic but unsupported survival trick.

The infected threat and any infected-specific official broadcasts are explicitly fictional. Real preparedness categories and general emergency behaviour will be checked against the Ready.gov and CDC sources listed in `CREATIVE_DIRECTION.md` before narration is locked.
