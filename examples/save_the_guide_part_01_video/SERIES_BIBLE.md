# Save the Guide — Series Bible

**Status:** Creative draft for user review. No story frames or reference images generated yet.

## Core premise

A male beginner on his first winter survival expedition must keep his injured female instructor alive after she falls through river ice and the group's main equipment pack is lost. She has the knowledge he lacks; he has the mobility she has lost. Neither can survive the situation alone.

## Format and tone

- **Series length:** Three connected parts.
- **Runtime:** Flexible. Each part should be as long as the story needs; no hard runtime cap or forced word-count cut.
- **Narration:** One continuous third-person narrator; short quoted dialogue remains in that same voice.
- **Tone:** Grounded, tense, humane, and ultimately hopeful.
- **Audience:** Viewers who enjoy survival situations, character-driven jeopardy, and practical problem-solving.
- **No:** supernatural elements, villains, gore, romance subplot, cartoonishly incompetent characters, or random animal attacks.
- **Antagonist:** Cold, terrain, fading time, impaired judgment, and the characters' own panic.

## Central dramatic engine

> **She knows what to do but cannot do it. He can do it but does not know how.**

The story begins with Evan depending on Mara's knowledge. It ends with Mara depending on Evan's judgment. The transfer of responsibility is the character arc.

## Theme and recurring line

**Theme:** Courage is not the absence of fear. It is choosing the next useful action while afraid.

**Mara's teaching:** “Panic spends energy. Spend it on the next thing.”

The line is introduced during an ordinary training failure, becomes necessary during the river rescue, fails Evan again while he builds the fire, and finally guides his own decisions when Mara can no longer direct him.

## Principal characters

### Mara Voss — survival instructor

- **Age:** 36.
- **Experience:** Fourteen winters guiding and teaching wilderness skills.
- **Personality:** Economical with words, observant, dryly humorous, demanding without being cruel.
- **Competence rule:** The accident must not make her look foolish. She checks the route and instructs Evan to wait, but moving water and snow conceal a weak patch that appearance alone cannot reliably reveal.
- **Role in the story:** Her early instructions save both of them. Her later cognitive decline forces Evan to stop waiting for instructions.
- **Physical story state:** Cold-water immersion, worsening hypothermia, and a right-knee injury that prevents walking. She must not make an implausible full recovery before evacuation.
- **Visual identity to develop:** Adult female proportions, a practical tied-back hairstyle, a distinctive rust-orange shell jacket, and calm but expressive eyes. The orange jacket is also a plot device.

### Evan Reed — beginner

- **Age:** 28.
- **Occupation:** Office worker; no useful wilderness or medical superpowers.
- **Personality:** Careful, self-critical, conflict-avoidant, and prone to freezing when events escape his plans.
- **Motivation:** He booked the course because he is ashamed of his hesitation during ordinary emergencies and wants to prove he can act.
- **Competence rule:** He is not stupid. He listens, makes understandable mistakes, and improves once he has a concrete action.
- **Role in the story:** He moves from waiting for the perfect answer to performing the next useful action.
- **Physical story state:** No convenient hidden injury or special skill. His costs are cold hands, exhaustion, fear, and responsibility for another person.
- **Visual identity to develop:** Adult male proportions, simple dark hair, a teal winter jacket, practical boots, and a mustard-coloured backpack. His face must support fear, concentration, and eventual determination without becoming a superhero caricature.

### Relationship

Professional trust, not romance. Mara never becomes a passive “damsel,” and Evan never becomes an instant expert. Their survival depends on communication, then on Evan applying what she taught him.

## Setting

**Blackpine Valley** is a fictional remote northern valley. The fictional setting lets the story control geography without making false claims about a real park or rescue agency.

- **Season:** Midwinter.
- **Daylight:** Late-afternoon accident, overnight shelter, dawn rescue.
- **Terrain:** A slow river side channel, a dense spruce stand, and a nearby open meadow.
- **Weather:** Light snow at the accident, worsening wind and snowfall overnight, clearing weather at dawn.
- **Communications:** No reliable mobile service. The satellite messenger is lost with Mara's pack. The prearranged lodge check-in is the backup rather than a magical instant rescue.

## Equipment logic

Mara carries group equipment; Evan carries his personal winter kit. This leaves them useful but insufficient resources after her pack is lost.

### Lost with Mara's pack

- Satellite messenger
- Group shelter
- Stove and group cooking equipment
- Mara's sleeping bag
- Most group food and emergency supplies

### Remaining with Evan

- Personal sleeping bag and foam pad
- Spare dry sweater and limited clothing
- Silver emergency bivy
- Throw rope
- Small fire kit and tinder
- Basic first-aid booklet
- Metal cup
- Compass with sighting mirror
- Emergency whistle
- Limited food and a phone with no service

The kit must not expand when the plot needs another object.

## Three-part arc

### Part 1 — The River

**Purpose:** Establish the relationship, trigger the accident, make Evan perform the rescue, and replace the external drowning danger with the less visible danger of hypothermia.

1. **In-medias-res hook:** Mara is in the river; Evan is frozen on the bank with the rope.
2. **Character setup:** A training failure introduces Evan's hesitation and Mara's “next thing” rule.
3. **Safety systems established:** Throw rope, spare clothing, equipment distribution, and the six o'clock lodge check-in.
4. **The crossing:** Mara tests the edge and crosses alone while Evan waits. A current-weakened patch hidden by snow gives way.
5. **The rescue:** Mara tells Evan to stay back. He throws from the bank, anchors himself, and pulls while she kicks horizontal and rolls clear.
6. **The new emergency:** Mara's wet clothing, injured knee, and exposure make walking out unrealistic.
7. **Limited shelter:** Evan uses his own pad, sleeping bag, dry layers, and bivy.
8. **Conflicting impulses:** Evan wants to run for the trailhead; Mara tells him to establish fire and protection first.
9. **False relief:** Evan starts the fire and interprets Mara's stillness as improvement.
10. **Cliffhanger:** Mara speaks confusedly about catching a bus and is no longer shivering. Evan realises the river rescue was only the beginning.

**Ending state:** Approximately nine minutes before the missed check-in. Mara is sheltered but confused and not shivering. Evan has a small fire, no communications, and no safe way to carry her out.

### Part 2 — The Longest Night

**Purpose:** Make survival depend on sustained care and judgment rather than one heroic action.

1. Evan initially interprets the lack of shivering as relief, then checks Mara's waterproof first-aid booklet and understands that confusion and reduced shivering can indicate worsening hypothermia.
2. He keeps her insulated, protected from wind, and as undisturbed as possible. He does not force food or drink while she is confused.
3. The six o'clock check-in passes. At the lodge, the missed call triggers the overdue-trip procedure. The route plan and clothing descriptions give rescuers a starting point, but weather and darkness prevent an immediate simple extraction.
4. Evan considers hiking for a signal. Between the dark, snow, Mara's condition, and the risk of leaving her, staying becomes the safer immediate decision.
5. The night becomes a series of small tasks: maintain the fire, keep the shelter intact, monitor Mara's responses, manage his own exhaustion, and avoid turning one emergency into two.
6. When Mara is alert and able to swallow safely, he can offer a warm non-alcoholic drink. Her returning shivering and clearer speech are encouraging, but she still needs evacuation and cannot walk on her knee.
7. At first light, search aircraft pass through the broader valley. The shelter under the spruces keeps them alive but prevents rescuers from seeing them.
8. **Cliffhanger:** Evan realises that warmth and visibility now conflict. He must create a signal away from the shelter without abandoning Mara or losing everything they have built.

**Ending state:** Mara is more responsive but still injured and vulnerable. Evan has a viable shelter, an overdue-trip search underway, and no way for rescuers to see them through the trees.

### Part 3 — The Last Signal

**Purpose:** Pay off the planning, clothing, and “next thing” setups; resolve both survival and Evan's arc.

1. At dawn, Mara is stable enough to speak clearly but cannot travel. Evan prepares insulation, fire, and instructions before leaving her for a short, necessary trip to the nearby meadow.
2. He uses dark branches to make a large contrasting X in the snow. At its centre he places Mara's rust-orange shell jacket—the same bright clothing described in the trip plan.
3. The first aircraft pass does not turn. Evan nearly runs after it, then stops and chooses the next useful action instead: stay visible and use the compass mirror deliberately.
4. He directs repeated flashes toward the aircraft and uses three whistle blasts when it is closer. The orange marker and contrast attract attention; the aircraft turns back.
5. Rescuers reach them. Mara receives professional care; she is not shown walking off her injuries.
6. In the helicopter, Mara tells Evan, “You saved the guide.” He answers, “You taught me how.” For the first time, he believes his own competence without pretending he was unafraid.

**Final meaning:** Evan did not become a different person. He became someone who could act while afraid.

## Setup and payoff ledger

| Early setup | Later payoff |
|---|---|
| “Spend it on the next thing.” | Evan uses it at the river, at the fire, and when the first signal appears to fail. |
| Throw-rope demonstration | Evan uses the rope from the bank instead of creating a second victim. |
| Mara checks in with the lodge at six | The missed call initiates the overdue-trip response. |
| Mara carries group gear; Evan carries personal gear | Her lost pack explains their limited but not nonexistent resources. |
| Mara wears a rust-orange shell | The jacket becomes the visible centrepiece of the dawn signal. |
| Evan's failed training fire | His successful emergency fire is a character payoff, not a new skill appearing from nowhere. |
| Shelter beneath dense spruces | It protects them from wind but conceals them from search aircraft. |
| Compass instruction | The sighting mirror becomes an active signalling tool in Part 3. |

## Continuity rules

1. Mara's right-knee injury begins at the ice edge and persists through rescue.
2. Her confusion and shivering state must track the progression of cold exposure and rewarming; she cannot alternate randomly between coherent and incoherent for drama.
3. Evan's clothing remains fundamentally consistent, with gradual snow, soot, and exhaustion rather than costume changes.
4. Mara's wet outer layers are removed in the shelter sequence; the orange jacket remains with them and is available for Part 3.
5. The lost pack and everything inside it do not reappear without a deliberate later story event.
6. The fire remains outside the insulated shelter and away from flammable material.
7. The river, shelter, and meadow geography must remain fixed once reference images are approved.
8. Rescue follows the prearranged plan and visible signals; no unexplained helicopter arrival.
9. Survival information stays subordinate to the human story and avoids presenting the video as a substitute for professional training.

## Creative guardrails

- Do not add a bear, wolf pack, stalker, or mystery figure to manufacture a cliffhanger.
- Do not make Mara careless merely to justify the accident.
- Do not make Evan suddenly perform advanced medical procedures.
- Do not use exact survival times or medical claims unless supported by the research and required by the scene.
- Do not let either character act against established conditions just because a frame would look exciting.
- Every part needs a decision or revelation at its ending, not an arbitrary interruption.
